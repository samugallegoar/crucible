SMA 10/30 crossover

SPY - 2015-01-01 to 2025-12-30 - starting capital $100,000

SMA 10/30 crossover turned $100,000 into $227,474 (+127.5%, 7.8% a year) on SPY from 2015-01-02 to 2025-12-30; buying and holding SPY would have made $402,665 (+302.7%, 13.5% a year).

VerdictNot conclusive: it made money in this one backtest and earned less than buy-and-hold; the evidence is too thin to tell skill from luck (see the warnings). Treat it as an idea worth testing further, not a basis for real money.

What to try next

  • Try a different period (for example the ten years before the start date, or one that includes a crash) and see whether the behaviour holds. A rule that only works in one window is a coincidence.
  • Run the same rules on other liquid instruments. A real effect should show up beyond one ticker.
  • Re-run with conservative costs (5 bps slippage, 2 bps commission) to see how much of the result survives worse fills.
  • Check that the result does not hinge on exact numbers: try 10 -> 8 and 12; 30 -> 22 and 38. If small changes flip the outcome the result is fragile. Each variant counts as a trial and lowers the deflated Sharpe, which is the honest price of looking.
  • Compare against buy-and-hold on risk, not just return: ask whether the smaller drawdown is worth the lower return for you.

The rules, in words

  • SMA 10/30 crossover: trades long (buy), deciding once per bar from that bar's close.
  • Buy when the 10-bar simple moving average of the closing price crosses above the 30-bar simple moving average of the closing price.
  • Sell when the 10-bar simple moving average of the closing price crosses below the 30-bar simple moving average of the closing price.
  • Each position uses all of its share of the capital (no leverage).
  • With several symbols the capital is split equally between them and each is traded on its own signal.
